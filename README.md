## 📄 Working Papers

[**Do More Appointments Lead to Shorter Waits and Better Patient Experience? Evidence from NHS Primary Care in England**](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.29.26359221v1)<br>
*Rohan Joseph, Harsh Gupta, Michael Keoghan, Shaun Danielli, and Andrew Scott*

*Abstract:* Across all 42 Integrated Care Boards in England (2018-2024), higher appointment volume was not associated with shorter waits or lower patient dissatisfaction, and shorter waits was not associated with lower dissatisfaction either. Performance frameworks that judge primary care productivity through these metrics need review.


## 🚧 Work in Progress

**Are Longer Waits Associated with Lower Satisfaction and Worse Recovery? Evidence from Hip and Knee Replacement in England**<br>
*Rohan Joseph, Harsh Gupta, Michael Keoghan, Shaun Danielli, and Andrew Scott*

*Abstract:* Linking referral-to-treatment waits to patient-reported outcomes across 240,000+ hip and knee replacements (2017-2023), longer waits were associated with higher post-operative dissatisfaction and smaller patient-reported functional improvements. In a focused clinical pathway, waits are associated with patient welfare, not just access.

**Intrinsic Capacity and the Economics of Healthy Ageing**<br>
*Julian Ashwin, Yannis Kastis, Rohan Joseph, and Andrew Scott*

*Abstract:* Using the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (Waves 1-9, 2002-2019), higher intrinsic capacity predicts more employment, less retirement and benefit receipt, lower mortality, and higher life satisfaction, across and within individuals and net of a deficit-based frailty index. The two are related but distinct,  and both bear on public finances.


## ⭐ Projects

📊 **The Capability-Use Gap in Occupational AI Diffusion**<br>
*Independent Research* · [Reproducible Codebase](https://github.com/Rohan-Joseph-2002/ai-occupation-adoption-gap-analysis) · [Live Dashboard](https://ai-occupation-adoption-gap-dashboard.streamlit.app/)

*Overview:* Merging Anthropic, OpenAI, O*NET, and BLS data across 709 occupations, realised AI use (0.082) sits far below measured capability (0.548), a mean gap of 0.466 that widens with digital-information and interpersonal work and narrows with physical work. The occupations most exposed to AI are not the ones where it has actually landed.

📉 **Latent Trend Discipline and Forecast Uncertainty in U.S. Unemployment**<br>
*Independent Research* · [Reproducible Codebase](https://github.com/Rohan-Joseph-2002/bayesian-unemployment-forecasting-analysis)

*Overview:* Comparing six Bayesian state-space specifications for monthly U.S. unemployment (FRED, 1948-2020), the semi-local trend selected by 26-window rolling cross-validation cut RMSE 7% and interval width 15% against local-linear at unchanged coverage, and RMSE 4.6% against auto-ARIMA. Trend discipline drives accuracy and uncertainty alike.

Many projects rely on **licensed, restricted, or locally derived datasets**. Where raw data cannot be shared, I prioritise:

- Transparent pipeline structure
- Clear schema and transformation logic
- Reproducible workflows with validation checks
- Public-data examples where possible

The goal is to make **methods and reasoning inspectable**, even when data is not.
